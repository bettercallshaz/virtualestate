01 · Before you commit

Cost, risk, and return — in that order.

Every agent, developer, and owner asks some version of the same three things: what does it cost, what could go wrong, and will it actually help sell or lease the unit. Here's the honest answer to each.

01

Will this actually bring in more serious buyers, or just look nice?

A walkthrough someone can explore on their own time pre-qualifies them before they book a viewing. The people who do show up already know they want the unit — fewer wasted viewings, more serious conversations, not just a better-looking listing.

02

Does it replace my photos, floor plans, or drone footage?

No — it works alongside them. Most people still skim photos before opening a 3D tour. The walkthrough is what a serious buyer opens next, not a replacement for what you already run.

03

Will it load fast on a buyer's phone, or frustrate them?

Every tour is optimised and mobile-checked before delivery. If it doesn't hold up on a phone screen, it doesn't go out — that's not a risk you're carrying on a slow launch.

04

My unit has a lot of glass, mirrors, or reflective surfaces — will that look broken?

Reflective and glass-heavy interiors are the hardest capture in this category, and they're planned for during the scan and cleanup rather than left as visible artifacts around windows and doorways. If a space is too difficult to do justice to, you'll hear that upfront — not after paying for it.

05

Is this worth it for every listing, or just the flagship units?

Honestly, not every listing. It earns its cost where the space itself is the selling point — larger units, premium interiors, show galleries, whole developments — anywhere a buyer's sense of the layout changes the decision to view or to buy. I'll say so if a listing doesn't need it.

06

What do I actually walk away owning?

One self-contained file, fully branded to you, with no subscription attached and no dependency on a third-party platform staying online. It's a fixed production cost, not a recurring one — the risk sits with the file working, not with a monthly bill.

07

How long does this actually take, start to finish?

On-site capture is typically from one hour to four hours, depending on the coverage of the scan. From that scan to a finished, branded file in your hands is measured in days, not weeks — and you'll get a firm timeline before I start, not an "it depends" after.

08

What happens to the file if my website changes, or years from now?

Nothing — that's the point of owning it outright. It's a standalone file, not a hosted link on someone else's platform. If your website changes, you re-embed the same file. If a platform shuts down, it doesn't affect you, because you were never dependent on one.

Three formats get used for property listings today, and they solve different problems. This is an honest breakdown of where each one fits — including when the simplest option is genuinely the right call.

Best for
